### 4.4. *Combined tense/aspect oppositions*

\[pp. 78–82\]

<mark>Written Arabic</mark>
- two verb forms - Perfective and Imperfective
- absence of temporal adverbs:
  - perfective forms: perfective and past
  - imperfective forms: imperfective and present

But: Imperfective forms can be interpreted as future and perfective when there is a future time adverbial

And: Perfective forms can be interpreted as having relative past time reference but be in the absolute future:

imperfective X + when + perfective Y – X will happen when Y has happened 

X happening when Y happened

X happened Y happening – X happened in order that Y would happen

Perfective = relative past
Imperfective = relative non-past

### 4.5. *Aspect and time reference in tenseless languages*

\[pp. 82–4]]

<mark>Yoruba</mark>, <mark>Igbo</mark> (West African):
- Some verbs are lexically imperfective (ie. ‘stative’ verbs).
- Other verbs are lexically perfective (ie. ‘non-stative’ verbs).
- There is an imperfective marker (Yoruba *ń*, Igbo *ná*) which can precede a lexically perfective verb, yielding a progressive/habitual construction 
- There are no explicit tense markers, but by default (eg. in the absence of a temporal adverbial):
  - Imperfective forms have **present** time reference.
  - Perfective forms have **past** time reference.

<mark>Proto-Indo-European</mark> may have worked in a similar way:
- There were two basic verbal categories – imperfective and perfective (traditionally known as ‘present’ and ‘aorist’ respectively).
- Imperfective verbs had default present time reference; perfective verbs had default past time reference.
- The so-called ‘imperfect’ verb forms were a later development, usually involving adding some kind of explicit past tense marker to an imperfective verb, hence the characterisation of the imperfect as ‘present in the past’.
