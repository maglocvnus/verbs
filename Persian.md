# Persian


Comrie (1976: 86 – *Aspect and voice*):
- *zan raft* ‘the woman went’, lit. woman.NOM went
- *pesar ketāb rā xarid* ‘the boy bought the book’, lit. boy.NOM book.ACC bought 

Comrie (1976):
- Persian has an invariable prefix *mi-* indicating imperfective aspect (p.88)
- The imperfective is *marked* in Persian (p. 114)
- (p. 121): In the Present Tense, apart from these few verbs, all Persian verbs occur only with the prefix *mi-*.


Present tense:
- *mi-xânam* ‘I read, I am reading’

Past tense:
- *mi-xândam* ‘I used to read, I was reading’
- *ketâb râ xândam* ‘I read the book’
- *ketâb râ mi-xândam* ‘I was reading the book, I used to read the book’
- raftam ‘I went’
- xordam ‘I ate’
- neveštam ‘I wrote’
- mi-raftam ‘I was writing, used to write’
- mi-xordam ‘I was eating, I used to eat’
- mi-neveštam ‘I was writing, I used to write’

personal endings next

The personal endings for verbs are:
- \[1sg\] `-am`
- \[2sg\] `-i`
- \[3sg\] `-ad` – only before a present verb stem, otherwise null
- \[1pl\] `-im`
- \[2pl\] `-id`
- \[3pl\] `-and`

A Persian verb has two distinct stems – a present stem, and a past stem.

Given one stem it is not usually possible to predict the other.

For example:
- gir-, gereft- 'take, took'
- nevis-, nevešt- 'write, wrote'
- deh-, dād- 'give, gave'
- kon-, kard- ‘make/do’
- dār-, dāšt-,  ‘have’
- bin-, did- ‘see’
- rav-, raft- ‘go’
- šav-, šod- ‘become’

Verb stems are perfective by default. Use the prefix *mi-* to turn them into imperfective stems.

Present indicative: mi + present stem + personal ending
- negative: ne + mi + present stem + personal ending

Present subjunctive: be + present stem + personal ending
- negative: ne + be + present stem + personal ending

Past perfective: past stem + personal ending

Past imperfective: mi + past stem + personal ending
