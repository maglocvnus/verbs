p. 304

“The category of tense has to do with time-relations in so far as these are expressed by systematic grammatical contrasts.”

p. 305

“The essential characteristic of the category of tense is that it relates the time of the action, event or state of affairs referred to in the sentence to the time of utterance (the time of utterance being ‘now’).”

p. 306

“The major tense distinction in English is undoubtedly that which is traditionally described as an opposite of ‘past’ versus ‘present’: e.g. *They jump*: *They jumped*. But this is best regarded as a contrast of ‘past’ v. ‘non-past’.”


John Lyons (1968) – *Introduction to Theoretical Linguistics*. §7.5. ‘Tense, mood and aspect’.
